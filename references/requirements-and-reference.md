# Progressive Requirements and Reference Direction

Read this reference when the user has not yet approved a website structure/visual direction, or when they want the site repositioned.

## Progressive intake

Do not begin with a consolidated questionnaire. First identify only the broad website direction. Offer a few starting points without treating them as exhaustive:

- individual portfolio or résumé;
- personal brand, creator, consultant, or service promotion;
- company, studio, product, or brand presentation;
- campaign, event, content hub, or another custom direction.

Let the user choose, combine, or rename the direction. Never infer “visual creator portfolio” merely because that was the origin of the workflow.

After positioning, ask one small follow-up batch at a time. Prefer two to four tightly related decisions that the user can answer naturally in one or two sentences. A useful sequence is:

1. positioning and what is being presented;
2. current goal, audience, and primary visitor action;
3. essential content and likely future expansion;
4. benchmark/reference and visual direction;
5. language, identity, conversion/contact paths, and delivery boundary;
6. production assets, requested only when they are about to be used.

Skip any item already answered. If the user is still exploring, recommend a direction and explain it briefly instead of demanding certainty. Do not ask for all materials at once, and do not make asset readiness a prerequisite for establishing the concept.

## Direction-specific discovery

Use the selected direction to decide what matters:

- **Portfolio or résumé:** strengths, representative work, role, project formats, employment or collaboration goal.
- **Personal brand or service:** expertise, offer, proof, audience problem, trust signals, booking/contact path, content strategy.
- **Company or brand presentation:** company/product positioning, customer segments, offerings, differentiators, credibility, sales/contact path, stakeholder approvals.
- **Campaign, event, or content hub:** campaign objective, time window, conversion event, content cadence, registration or destination flow.

Do not invent clients, performance claims, awards, testimonials, certifications, or company facts.

## Information required before the final brief

Collect the following progressively rather than as a rigid questionnaire:

1. **Website positioning** — chosen site type; subject, person, brand, company, product, or campaign being presented.
2. **Current goal** — showcase work, find clients, sell services, publish tutorials, or build authority.
3. **Future goal** — content/tutorial expansion, additional services, bilingual content, shop, newsletter, or other later additions.
4. **Audience and desired action** — who should visit and what they should do next.
5. **Content inventory** — relevant work, services, products, company information, proof, articles, campaign material, media, or other content for the selected direction.
6. **Page content** — propose sections only after the direction is known; do not impose portfolio sections on other site types.
7. **Benchmark/reference websites (mandatory)** — ask whether the user has one or more comparison sites. Accept direct URLs, full-page long screenshots, or complete scroll-through screen recordings. Record explicitly when there is no benchmark rather than omitting the question.
8. **Visual direction** — what they like or dislike about each reference; base color, openness to alternatives, and desired level of experimentation.
9. **Language and copy** — Chinese/English/bilingual; whether personal, company, product, service, or project copy is ready or needs drafting.
10. **Identity/contact** — logo, portrait, email, WeChat/QR, social profiles, tutorial links.
11. **Deliverable boundary** — Figma only, Figma then Astro, or existing-stage revision; local-only versus deployment later.

Do not ask again for facts already supplied. If the user says later materials will follow, proceed with deliberate placeholders and produce a missing-asset checklist.

## Reference-site analysis

Inspect the actual experience, not only its colors. When possible, review:

- full-page section sequence and approximate length;
- header behavior and hero composition;
- heading scale, density, whitespace, alignment, and grid;
- relevant content-card ratio and browsing pattern;
- motion, hover, scroll, modal, accordion, and mobile navigation behavior;
- desktop/mobile adaptation rather than proportional shrinking;
- footer/contact conversion path;
- elements that belong to the reference brand and must not be copied.

When asking for a reference, tell the user that a direct website link is welcome and that they may instead provide a full-page long screenshot or a complete scroll-through screen recording. Prefer a recording when motion, hover, scroll, transitions, or responsive behavior are important.

If a URL is blocked, incomplete, login-gated, or highly animated, request a long screenshot or screen recording. Analyze it first and report the structural/visual principles before redesigning. Do not immediately edit the existing design based on a partial impression.

## Direction summary

Before opening Figma, produce a compact direction containing:

- one-sentence positioning;
- primary visitor action;
- proposed section order;
- visual keywords and palette direction;
- desktop and mobile content strategy;
- required interactions;
- confirmed assets and missing assets;
- statements that must remain provisional.

Reference inspiration must be translated into the user's own identity or brand. Match the level of polish and interaction, not another site's logo, exact text, artwork, or distinctive proprietary composition.

## Asset checklist template

Request only the groups relevant to the selected direction, and ask for them progressively as production approaches:

- Brand: transparent logo, preferred wordmark, favicon if available.
- Hero/showreel: landscape and portrait video or poster options.
- Portfolio/projects when applicable: latest final video, cover still, title, short description, category, role, duration, commercial status.
- Personal profile when applicable: 1–2 portrait options and approved facts for biography.
- Company/brand when applicable: approved company profile, product/service information, differentiators, team or facility images, proof and legal wording.
- Campaign/event when applicable: campaign dates, key message, CTA destination, registration or conversion requirements, approved promotional media.
- Contact: email, WeChat ID and QR, social URLs.
- Tutorial/content: cover, title, destination link.
- Optional: process images, client marks, credits, legal/copyright notes.

Avoid requesting production-process material when the user has explicitly deferred or excluded it.
